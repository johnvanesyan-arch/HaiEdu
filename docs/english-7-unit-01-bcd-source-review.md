## Reading passages

### ENG7-U01-DB-READ-A

Questions: ENG7-U01-DB-21, ENG7-U01-DB-22.

A school club planned a clothes-swap afternoon. Its first notice asked people to bring fashionable clothes. Several students were unsure: did their clothes have to be popular that month?

The club rewrote the notice. It now asked for clean clothes in good condition, with the size written on a card. People could choose according to fit and personal preference. The club did not select one winning style.

Students who had misunderstood the first notice now knew what they could bring. A clear description of the event was more useful than a vague fashion label.

### ENG7-U01-DB-READ-B

Questions: ENG7-U01-DB-23, ENG7-U01-DB-24.

The Fashion and Design Chamber of Armenia describes Inga Manukyan's path to LOOM Weaving. During her art studies, Armenian carpet weaving gave her ideas for clothing. Later, with encouragement from her sister, she developed that idea further. The sisters started LOOM Weaving in 2014.

The profile connects their knitwear with handmade techniques and modern style. It shows one way a traditional craft can inspire clothing made for the present.

Primary source: https://www.fdc.am/designers/5 · retrieved 2026-10-22. This is an original short paraphrase, not a recommendation to buy the products.

### ENG7-U01-MB-READ-A

Questions: ENG7-U01-MB-21, ENG7-U01-MB-22.

For a class project, Lilit photographed three people wearing the same borrowed coat. She expected their written descriptions of it to be alike. Instead, one person praised the deep pockets, another liked the bright lining, and the third said the coat was difficult to move in.

Lilit kept all three comments beside the photographs. The project was about people's different experiences, so she did not replace the comments with one score for the coat. Readers could see what mattered to each person.

### ENG7-U01-MB-READ-B

Questions: ENG7-U01-MB-23, ENG7-U01-MB-24.

According to the Fashion and Design Chamber of Armenia, SHABEEG uses clothing to share Armenian heritage. Nature, architecture and traditional dress provide ideas for its designs. Its products include T-shirts as well as other clothes and bags.

The profile describes a clear design sequence: the pictures are first drawn by hand and then turned into digital images. Traditional sources of inspiration and a later digital step are both part of the account.

### ENG7-U01-MC-READ-A

Questions: ENG7-U01-MC-21, ENG7-U01-MC-22.

A class prepared a guide to costumes for visitors who would not see the display. The first draft said only that each costume was beautiful or unusual. When a classmate read it without the photographs, those words did not help her imagine the clothes.

The writers added colours, shapes and parts such as collars and sleeves. They kept opinions in a separate final sentence. For one jacket, they described a short green body with long yellow sleeves. The revised guide gave readers concrete information before offering an opinion.

### ENG7-U01-MC-READ-B

Questions: ENG7-U01-MC-23, ENG7-U01-MC-24.

The Fashion and Design Chamber of Armenia describes two connected activities at the Teryan Cultural Center. One is carefully recreating traditional Armenian clothing with advice from experts who study culture and traditions. Another is making modern clothes and accessories that use traditional elements.

The center also offers opportunities to learn traditional crafts. In this account, preserving older designs and using cultural ideas in present-day work are connected, but they are not described as exactly the same activity.

**Speaker**

For my class presentation, I first chose a shirt with large shiny decorations. At rehearsal, my partner kept looking at the shirt instead of the drawings I was explaining. I wanted the drawings to get the attention, so I chose a plain dark top for the presentation. I still like the shiny shirt, and I will wear it at our club party afterwards. ### ENG7-U01-MB-AUDIO-A

**Speaker**

When our group met for the first time, Daniel said very little. I thought he did not want to help with our presentation. Later, he showed us a page of notes. He had written down all the jobs we needed to do and noticed that nobody had offered to prepare the introduction. He offered to do it. I realised he had been listening and thinking. Next time, I will give a quiet member time to share an idea before deciding that they are not interested.

### ENG7-U01-MB-AUDIO-B

Filename: `eng7-u01-mb-audio-b-v1.mp3`  
Questions: ENG7-U01-MB-26, ENG7-U01-MB-28.

**Narrator**

Listen to three students discussing how to get feedback on a club T-shirt design. You will hear each speaker's number.

**Speaker 1**

Speaker One. Let's give everyone a card. They can write one thing they like and one thing they would change, without writing their name.

**Speaker 2**

Speaker Two. I want to show the design on a real T-shirt first. A small drawing may not show how the picture looks when someone wears it.

**Speaker 3**

Speaker Three. We should ask a clear question about the lettering. Can people read the club name from across the room? That would help us choose its size.

### ENG7-U01-MC-AUDIO-A

Filename: `eng7-u01-mc-audio-a-v1.mp3`  
Questions: ENG7-U01-MC-25, ENG7-U01-MC-27.

**Speaker**

For our class survey, I first asked, 'Which clothes do you like best?' Several classmates said that it depended on what they were doing. So I changed the survey. I asked about a walk, a family celebration and a day at home separately. The answers were easier to understand because each question named an occasion. For the walk, I also left a space for people to explain their choice. Many wrote about being able to move comfortably. I learned that one general question had hidden useful differences.

### ENG7-U01-MC-AUDIO-B

Filename: `eng7-u01-mc-audio-b-v1.mp3`  
Questions: ENG7-U01-MC-26, ENG7-U01-MC-28.

**Narrator**

Listen to three students suggesting ways to settle a disagreement about a costume for a school play. You will hear each speaker's number.

**Speaker 1**

Speaker One. We could try both ideas in a short rehearsal. Then we would know which one lets the actor move more easily, instead of only guessing.

**Speaker 2**

Speaker Two. Let's remember what the character needs to do in the scene. We all want the audience to understand the character, even though we prefer different designs.

**Speaker 3**

Speaker Three. Perhaps we can decide on the shape first and discuss the colours afterwards. Trying to settle every detail at once is making the discussion harder.
